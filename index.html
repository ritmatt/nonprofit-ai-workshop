1 Start Here — Live Demo Slide 11
⌄

The difference between a useful result and a useless one is almost always the prompt.

Weak prompt — what most people type
Help me with a board meeting.
Strong prompt — with context and specifics
Act as an experienced nonprofit executive assistant. I'm preparing for a board meeting for a small nonprofit that provides after-school tutoring for low-income students in the Atlanta area. The board cares most about: donor retention, program outcomes, and volunteer capacity. Please help me: 1. Organize a clear meeting agenda 2. Identify the key decisions the board needs to make 3. Draft a short follow-up summary template for after the meeting Do not invent facts. Ask clarifying questions if anything is unclear.
What changed?
✅ Role — "Act as an experienced nonprofit executive assistant"
✅ Context — org type, mission, location
✅ Audience — what the board cares about
✅ Specific outputs — agenda, decisions, follow-up template
✅ Guardrails — "Do not invent facts. Ask if unclear."
The difference is not magic — it's context.
2 AI Safety Rules Slide 14
⌄
Green — Try it
Brainstorming · Drafting generic communications · Rewriting for clarity · Summarizing public information · Creating checklists
Yellow — Pause and review
Donor communications · Grant narratives · HR-related content · Program recommendations · Financial summaries
Red — Do not paste
Confidential donor data · Client case details · Medical or legal advice · Passwords or credentials · Sensitive employee information

Paid accounts (~$20/mo) have stronger data protections. When in doubt, ask your leadership before using AI with sensitive information.

3 Hands-On Prompt Starters Slide 22
⌄

Your first prompt doesn't need to be perfect. The goal is to make it better than "help me with this."

For writing & communications
I run a nonprofit that [brief description]. Write me [specific thing] for [specific audience]. Tone should be [warm/professional/urgent]. Length: about [X] words.
For decision-making
I'm the [role] of [org type]. I'm trying to decide [decision]. The options are [A] and [B]. Help me think through the tradeoffs and what questions I should be asking.
For research
What are best practices for [topic] among small nonprofits? I'm specifically interested in [angle]. Give me 5 concrete examples with brief explanations.
For summarizing notes or documents
Here are my notes from [meeting/document]. Please: 1. Summarize the key decisions 2. List all action items with owners 3. Flag any unresolved questions
For donor thank-you emails
Help me draft a warm donor thank-you email. Use this information: [add donation context, campaign, or impact]. Keep it sincere, concise, and human. Do not invent specific results or numbers.
For volunteer onboarding
Act as a volunteer coordinator for a small nonprofit. Create a simple onboarding checklist for new volunteers. Include what they need before, during, and after their first shift. Make it practical and easy to follow.
For thinking through a decision
Act as a thoughtful decision partner. I am considering [decision]. Help me think through options, risks, tradeoffs, people affected, and next steps. Ask clarifying questions before giving a recommendation.
Keys to a good prompt
Give context: Who you are, what your org does, who the audience is
Be specific: Length, tone, format, what to include or avoid
Iterate: If it's 70% right, ask AI to refine — don't start over
Ask follow-ups: "Make it shorter." "Change the tone." "Add a call to action."
4 5 Things to Try This Week Slide 24
⌄
1
Paste your last board meeting notes into an AI tool. Ask it to summarize key decisions and extract all action items with owners.
2
Draft three versions of your next newsletter opening paragraph — then pick your favorite and edit it into your voice.
3
Research what similar organizations in your sector are doing to solve a challenge you're currently facing.
4
Ask AI to help you think through a decision you've been sitting on — describe the situation and ask it to help identify what's really at stake.
5
Create a volunteer onboarding checklist by describing your current informal process and asking AI to turn it into a structured document.

Pick one. Do it before Friday. Learning happens by doing — not by watching.

5 Recommended Tools Slide 17
⌄

Start with whatever you already have access to. Don't start by shopping for new apps.

Microsoft Copilot — copilot.microsoft.com · Included in many M365 plans.
ChatGPT — chatgpt.com · Free tier. Most widely used.
Claude — claude.ai · Free tier. Strong for writing and analysis.
Perplexity — perplexity.ai · Free tier. Great for research with sources.
Google Gemini — gemini.google.com · Free with Google Workspace.

Paid accounts (~$20/mo) have stronger data protections — worth it for regular organizational use.
